Deep mods: VESC, custom batteries, custom hub motors

When firmware and P-settings run out, this is the next level. Replace the controller with an open-source VESC, build a battery pack around the cells and voltage you actually want, and fit a hub motor that can use the power. Each tab has the parts, the steps and how well every platform takes the mod.

What a VESC gives you

A VESC (Vedder Electronic Speed Controller) is an open-source motor controller you configure from a laptop or phone with VESC Tool. Instead of a hidden P-settings menu, you get direct control of motor current, battery current, throttle curve, regen strength, field weakening and voltage cutoffs, plus live logging of every ride. FOC (field-oriented control) makes even cheap hub motors run smoother and quieter than the square-wave controllers most scooters ship with.

  • Best for: locked-firmware scooters (Xiaomi 4, Ninebot G2, NIU, Navee), dual-motor performance builds, anyone who wants data.
  • Cost: $120–$550 for the ESC, plus a Hall throttle ($15), display or Bluetooth module ($20–$80) and wiring.
  • Trade-off: you lose the stock dashboard, app and usually the lights and lock unless you rewire them.

    Why build your own pack

    Stock packs are built to a price with mid-current cells and a BMS sized for stock power. A custom pack lets you pick high-drain cells, add parallel groups for current and range, and raise the series count for speed. It is also the most dangerous mod on this site: lithium cells do not forgive shorts, punctures or bad welds.

    • Best for: anyone who has already upgraded a controller and is hitting voltage sag, or wants more range without an external pack.
    • Cost: $3–$6 per 21700 cell. A 16S4P P45B pack is roughly $350 in cells plus $60–$120 for a smart BMS, nickel, wire and insulation.
    • Trade-off: requires a spot welder and patience. Buy a pre-built pack from a reputable builder if you have never done it.

      When the motor is the bottleneck

      Firmware and controller tunes push more current through the stock motor, which turns into heat. A bigger hub motor has more copper and more magnet, so it makes more torque at the same current and survives sustained high speed. Motor swaps are where a 25 km/h commuter becomes a real 40 km/h scooter, and where a dual-motor performance scooter gains the last 20 %.

      • Best for: Ninebot Max and Xiaomi owners who tuned firmware and now overheat; performance builds moving to 72 V.
      • Cost: $80–$150 for commuter hubs, $200–$500 per motor for 10–11-inch performance hubs.
      • Trade-off: more unsprung weight, fork or swingarm compatibility, and you will need brakes to match.

        Before you touch anything

        ⚖️ Know your local law

        Most jurisdictions limit e-scooter speed and power on public roads. A tuned scooter can be reclassified as a motor vehicle that needs registration, insurance and a licence. Tune for private land, closed tracks or where it is explicitly allowed.

        🛑 Brakes before speed

        Every tuning plan on this site should start with brakes and tires. Stopping distance grows with the square of speed: going from 25 km/h to 35 km/h nearly doubles the distance you need to stop.

        🔥 Respect the battery

        Lithium packs can catch fire when cells are punctured, over-discharged or charged with the wrong charger. Never bypass the BMS. If you are not confident with a spot welder, buy a pre-built pack from a reputable seller.

        💾 Back up before flashing

        Dump and save your stock firmware before flashing anything. Keep an ST-Link programmer on hand for Ninebot and Xiaomi units so a bad flash is an inconvenience, not a dead scooter.

        🌡️ Watch the temperature

        Raised motor current means more heat. After a tune, touch the motor after a hard ride: if you cannot hold your hand on it, back the current off or upgrade the motor.

        🪖 Gear up

        Above 30 km/h a bicycle helmet is no longer enough. Full-face or downhill helmets, gloves and wrist guards are the minimum for a tuned performance scooter.
